## 1. Basil builds a house of care for the sick and the poor (c. 372)
At Caesarea in Cappadocia (now Kayseri, Turkey), Bishop Basil built a large complex named after him, the Basileias. It had a hostel for travellers, housing for the poor, the old and the sick, and a place where lepers were housed, fed and treated. A church and a monastery stood beside it. It may be the first hospital for the public of which evidence survives.

- **Uncertain:** the exact years of building are not known. It is usually dated to the 370s, about 372.
- **Tied to:** "For I was hungry and you gave Me something to eat, I was thirsty and you gave Me something to drink, I was a stranger and you took Me in," (Matthew 25:35)
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia, Basileias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basileias) · [Hektoen International, "Reconstructing the world's first hospital: the Basiliad"](https://hekint.org/2017/02/24/reconstructing-the-worlds-first-hospital-the-basiliad/)

## 2. Fabiola nurses the sick with her own hands (c. 390–399)
Fabiola, a wealthy Roman Christian woman, gave her fortune to the poor and founded a hospital in Rome, the first in the West according to Jerome, who knew her. He wrote that she gathered the sick from the streets, carried them on her own shoulders, washed their wounds and fed them with her own hand. With Pammachius she also set up a home for strangers at Portus, the port of Rome. She died in 399.

- **Uncertain:** the hospital's year is given as 390 in some sources and 394 in others.
- **Tied to:** "I was naked and you clothed Me, I was sick and you looked after Me, I was in prison and you visited Me." (Matthew 25:36)
- **Sources:** [Jerome, Letter 77 to Oceanus (New Advent)](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001077.htm) · [Roman Letters, Jerome Letter 77](https://romanletters.org/letters/jerome/77/)

## 3. Benedict's Rule: care for the sick before all things (c. 530)
Benedict of Nursia wrote a Rule for his monks, probably at Monte Cassino in Italy. Its chapter on the sick begins with care for them "before all things and above all things," so that they are served as Christ himself. It quotes Matthew 25:36 and 25:40. The Rule went on to shape monasteries across Europe.

- **Uncertain:** the exact date and place are not known. About 530 is the usual estimate.
- **Tied to:** "And the King will reply, ‘Truly I tell you, whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers of Mine, you did for Me.’" (Matthew 25:40)
- **Sources:** [Catholic Encyclopedia, Rule of Saint Benedict](https://www.catholic.com/encyclopedia/Rule-of-Saint-Benedict) · [Rule of Benedict, ch. 36 "The Sick Brothers" (PALNI Press)](https://pressbooks.palni.org/ruleofbenedict/?p=153)

## 4. Elizabeth of Hungary serves at Marburg (1228)
Elizabeth, a Hungarian princess and widow of the ruler of Thuringia, had already built a 28-bed hospital below the Wartburg in 1226. After her husband died in 1227, she went to Marburg and spent what she had left on a hospital there (1228). There she nursed the sick and the poor herself, taking on the hardest work. She died in 1231.

- **Uncertain:** her death is dated 17 or 19 November 1231.
- **Tied to:** "Pure and undefiled religion before our God and Father is this: to care for orphans and widows in their distress, and to keep oneself from being polluted by the world." (James 1:27)
- **Sources:** [Pope Benedict XVI, general audience on St Elizabeth of Hungary, 20 October 2010](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2010/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20101020.html) · [Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), St. Elizabeth of Hungary](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia_(1913)/St._Elizabeth_of_Hungary)

## 5. The Daughters of Charity go out to the poor (1633)
In Paris in 1633, Vincent de Paul and Louise de Marillac founded the Daughters of Charity. Louise took the first young women into her own home on 29 November 1633. They were not enclosed as nuns were. They went out into the streets, homes and hospitals to serve the poor and the sick, the first such community of women devoted to active works of charity.

- **Uncertain:** Louise's birth year is given as 1591 (Britannica) or 1581 in some other sources.
- **Tied to:** "Little children, let us love not in word and speech, but in action and truth." (1 John 3:18)
- **Sources:** [Britannica, Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul](https://britannica.com/print/article/106528) · [Britannica, St. Louise de Marillac](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/365198) · [St. John's University, Louise de Marillac: the first Daughter of Charity](https://www.stjohns.edu/about/news/2020-03-30/louise-de-marillac-first-daughter-charity)

## 6. Florence Nightingale answers her call (1837–1860)
On 7 February 1837 Florence Nightingale recorded in her diary that God had called her to His service. Against her parents' wishes she became a nurse. In 1854 she led 38 women to the British army hospital at Scutari in the Crimean War, where she found filth, overcrowding and too few supplies. In 1860 she opened the Nightingale School of Nursing at St Thomas' Hospital in London, the first scientifically based nursing school.

- **Tied to:** "Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying: “Whom shall I send? Who will go for Us?” And I said: “Here am I. Send me!”" (Isaiah 6:8)
- **Sources:** [Britannica, 100 Women: Florence Nightingale](https://www.britannica.com/explore/100women/?p=404) · [Christian History Institute, events in the life of Florence Nightingale](https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/magazine/article/events-in-the-life-of-florence-nightingale)

## 7. Henry Dunant and the Red Cross (1859–1864)
Henry Dunant, a Swiss businessman who had helped found the YMCA, saw the Battle of Solferino in 1859, which left nearly 40,000 casualties. He organised help for the wounded of both armies. His book *A Memory of Solferino* (1862) called for volunteer relief societies in every country. In 1863 the committee that became the International Committee of the Red Cross was founded in Geneva, and the first Geneva Convention followed in 1864. He shared the first Nobel Peace Prize in 1901.

- **Honest note:** his exact role in founding the YMCA's world alliance is described differently by different sources.
- **Tied to:** "But a Samaritan on a journey came upon him, and when he saw him, he had compassion." (Luke 10:33)
- **Sources:** [Britannica, Henri Dunant](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Henri-Dunant) · [Wikipedia, Henry Dunant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Dunant)

## 8. Father Damien becomes a leper with the lepers (1873–1889)
Jozef De Veuster, a Belgian priest known as Father Damien, was the first to volunteer for the leprosy settlement at Kalawao on Molokaʻi, Hawaii. He arrived on 10 May 1873, when about 600 people lived there under forced quarantine. He dressed their sores, built homes, a church and a water supply, made coffins and dug graves. In December 1884 he found he had leprosy himself. He kept working and died there on 15 April 1889.

- **Tied to:** "Greater love has no one than this, that he lay down his life for his friends." (John 15:13)
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia, Father Damien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_Damien) · [Catholic World Report, the suffering and faith of Saint Damien of Molokai](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2020/07/31/the-suffering-and-faith-of-saint-damien-of-molokai/)

## 9. Mother Teresa and the Missionaries of Charity (1950)
Mother Teresa, a teacher in Calcutta (Kolkata), left her post in 1948 to live and work among the poorest in the city's slums. In 1950 she founded the Missionaries of Charity, whose sisters ran homes for orphans, care for people with leprosy, and a home for the dying (from 1952). She received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979 for "her work in bringing help to suffering humanity," and asked that the banquet money be given to the poor.

- **Honest note:** she was also criticised, including over the standard of medical care and pain relief in her homes for the dying. The Nobel Foundation's own page notes this. Seabass may judge whether this entry belongs.
- **Tied to:** "Blessed is the one who cares for the poor; the LORD will deliver him in the day of trouble." (Psalm 41:1)
- **Sources:** [Nobel Prize, Mother Teresa facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1979/teresa/facts/) · [Britannica, Missionaries of Charity](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/385551) · [EBSCO Research Starter, Mother Teresa awarded the Nobel Peace Prize](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/mother-teresa-awarded-nobel-peace-prize)

## 10. Cicely Saunders opens the first modern hospice (1967)
Cicely Saunders trained as a nurse, then as a social worker (almoner), then qualified as a doctor in 1957 at 39, all so that she could care better for the dying. After nearly 20 years of planning, she opened St Christopher's Hospice in south-east London in 1967, the first hospice for care, research and teaching. Her idea of "total pain" treats the body, the mind, the family and the spirit together. Her Christian faith underpinned her work, and she insisted the hospice serve people "of all faiths or of none." Hospices around the world grew from it.

- **Tied to:** "who comforts us in all our troubles, so that we can comfort those in any trouble with the comfort we ourselves have received from God." (2 Corinthians 1:4)
- **Sources:** [Royal College of Physicians, Dame Cicely Saunders](https://history.rcp.ac.uk/inspiring-physicians/dame-cicely-mary-strode-saunders) · [BMJ obituary, Dame Cicely Saunders](https://www.bmj.com/content/331/7510/238) · [Christianity Today, Cicely Saunders](https://christianitytoday.com/2009/04/dame-cicely-saunders-hospice-terminally-ill)
